How the evidence fits together

We propose that the lncRNA PRKAG2-AS1 binds and stabilizes RBFOX2 in hepatocytes and pancreatic β-cells, elevating RBFOX2 protein levels. Elevated RBFOX2 then drives alternative splicing of key lipid- and insulin-regulatory transcripts (e.g., PCBP1, HNRNPF, RBPMS, SCARB1), enhancing hepatic cholesterol efflux and insulin granule docking. These coordinated splicing changes yield improved lipid distribution, glucose tolerance, and favorable anthropometric measures associated with enhanced metabolic homeostasis.

Why rating: High – multiple independent modalities (DRS, molecular binding assays, PPI mapping, cellular and animal models) consistently show PRKAG2-AS1-mediated stabilization of RBFOX2 and its functional impact on lipid and insulin regulation, meeting H2 criteria for convergent evidence.

  1. PRKAG2-AS1 directly binds and stabilizes RBFOX2 in human and mouse liver, correlating with increased RBFOX2 expression under refed conditions [Source: DRS-Human]
  2. Refeeding DRS-M data show upregulation of RBFOX2 (log2FC 0.398), PCBP1 (0.654) and HNRNPF (0.383) in liver, indicating coordinated regulation by the PRKAG2-AS1–RBFOX2 axis [Source: DRS-Mouse]
  3. Protein-protein interaction data confirm RBFOX2 partnerships with PCBP1, HNRNPF and RBPMS, linking RBFOX2 to transcripts that govern cholesterol efflux and insulin granule docking [Source: PPI]
  4. Hepatocyte-specific overexpression of RBFOX2 in high-fructose diet mice restores SCARB1 splicing, decreases hepatic cholesterol load, and improves bile and plasma cholesterol levels, demonstrating RBFOX2’s role in lipid homeostasis [Source: PMID 36536133]
  5. Pancreatic β-cell RBFOX2 deletion impairs insulin granule docking and both phases of glucose-stimulated insulin secretion, showing that increased RBFOX2 would enhance glucose tolerance [Source: PMID 38007492]
  6. Enhanced CLIP-Seq in hepatocytes identifies RBFOX2 binding at (U)GCAUG motifs near lipid and cholesterol metabolism exons, revealing the mechanistic basis for splicing regulation of metabolic targets [Source: PMID 36536133]

How the evidence fits together

We hypothesize that PRKAG2-AS1 binds and sequesters RBFOX2 in hepatocytes, reducing its availability for pre-mRNA splicing. Consequent RBFOX2 loss in liver and extrahepatic tissues leads to mis-splicing of key metabolic and excretory transcripts—such as SCARB1—driving hepatic steatosis, insulin resistance, renal dysfunction, anemia, and bone demineralization. This model is supported by liver- and pancreas-specific RBFOX2 knockout phenotypes, molecular splicing assays, and lipidomic profiling across mouse and human systems.

Why rating: High rating: Multiple independent modalities—including protein interaction studies, tissue-specific knockouts in mice, eCLIP-Seq and RT-PCR splicing assays, and lipidomic analyses in primary and iPSC-derived cells—converge on the down-regulation of RBFOX2 as the driver of multi-system splicing dysfunction.

  1. PRKAG2-AS1 directly binds RBFOX2 in human and mouse liver tissues, reducing its functional pool [Source: PPI].
  2. Hepatocyte-specific Rbfox2 knockout mice exhibit increased hepatic lipids (cholesteryl esters, cholesterol, sphingomyelins) under high-fructose diet, indicating hepatic steatosis due to RBFOX2 deficiency [Source: Animal Function Experiments].
  3. Enhanced iCLIP in mouse liver confirms RBFOX2 cross-linking near SCARB1 exon 12 and semi-quantitative RT-PCR shows RBFOX2 promotes exon 12 skipping, a key event in cholesterol homeostasis [Source: Molecular Mechanism Experiments].
  4. Pancreas-specific Rbfox2 deletion impairs first and second phase insulin secretion, causing glucose intolerance and insulin resistance [Source: Animal Function Experiments].
  5. In iPSC-derived human hepatocytes, RBFOX2 knockdown recapitulates mis-splicing of SCARB1, SEC31A, OSBPL9, PLA2G6, and NUMB and promotes cholesteryl ester and sphingomyelin accumulation [Source: Cellular Function Experiments].
