Molecular Mechanism ExperimentsModerate
  • PMID 36536133 showed that RBFOX2 promotes skipping of SCARB1 exon 12 and regulates alternative splicing of key lipid metabolism genes like PLA2G6 in mouse liver. eiCLIP profiling revealed RBFOX2-bound transcripts enriched in lipoprotein receptor and cholesterol metabolism functions. Dietary high-fat and high-fructose feeding decreased full-length active RBFOX2 expression, connecting metabolic state to its splicing activity.
Cellular Function ExperimentsModerate
  • PMID 36536133 reported that RBFOX2 knockdown in iPSC-derived human hepatocytes increases cholesteryl esters and sphingomyelins and that RBFOX2-deficient mouse hepatocytes exhibit elevated lipid accumulation. Splice-switching of OSBPL9 exon 6 mimicked RBFOX2 deficiency effects by increasing ceramide, triglyceride, and phosphatidylcholine levels. These results underline RBFOX2’s role in controlling hepatocyte lipid composition.
Animal Function ExperimentsStrong
  • PMID 38302461 showed hepatocyte-specific RBFOX2 knockout mice have decreased blood cholesterol but increased hepatic cholesterol, bile acids, and other lipids. Multiple studies in PMID 36536133 confirmed that liver-specific RBFOX2 deletion reduces plasma cholesterol, alters liver lipid and bile acid profiles, and that RBFOX2 overexpression reverses these lipid changes including reduced hepatic and biliary cholesterol. Altogether, these animal models establish RBFOX2 as a critical regulator of systemic and hepatic lipid distribution.
Bioinformatic AnalysisModerate
  • PMID 36536133 found that RBFOX2 expression in mouse liver is transcriptionally regulated by feeding–fasting cycles. Bioinformatic mapping of mouse RBFOX2 targets revealed enrichment in human GWAS loci for lipid metabolism, LDL cholesterol, and triglycerides, linking RBFOX2 targets to human lipid phenotypes.
Answer to user query

Strong animal studies show that hepatocyte-specific RBFOX2 deletion lowers circulating cholesterol but causes hepatic accumulation of cholesterol, bile acids, and other lipids, whereas RBFOX2 overexpression reduces hepatic cholesterol and biliary cholesterol secretion, indicating RBFOX2 as a key regulator of liver lipid metabolism.

Conclusions
  1. RBFOX2 is transcriptionally regulated by feeding and fasting cycles in the liver.
  2. High-fat diet and high-fructose diet induced obesity are associated with decreased expression of the main RBFOX2 isoform in the liver.
  3. Diet-induced obesity is associated with decreased levels of full-length active RBFOX2 in the liver.
  4. In the liver, RBFOX2 cross-linked clusters are enriched in transcripts encoding proteins with phosphatidylcholine-sterol-O-acyltransferase activity, lipoprotein particle receptor binding, apolipoprotein receptor binding, and low-density lipoprotein particle receptor binding, suggesting a role for RBFOX2 in lipid metabolism.
  5. Human orthologs of mouse RBFOX2 targets detected by eiCLIP are enriched in genes implicated by genome-wide association studies in human lipid metabolism phenotypes, LDL cholesterol levels, and triglycerides.
  6. RBFOX2 promotes SCARB1 exon 12 skipping in mouse liver.
  7. RBFOX2 regulates alternative splicing of PLA2G6 in mouse liver.
  8. Liver-specific RBFOX2 knockout in male mice leads to a significant decrease in total cholesterol levels.
  9. In high-fructose diet-fed mice, liver-specific RBFOX2 ablation increases hepatic lipid content, including cholesteryl esters, cholesterol, and sphingomyelins.
  10. Liver-specific RBFOX2 knockout mice exhibit a shift in triglyceride composition favoring longer polyunsaturated fatty acid chains.
  11. RBFOX2 deficiency in human hepatocytes maintains alternative splicing changes in SCARB1, SEC31A, OSBPL9, PLA2G6, and NUMB transcripts.
  12. RBFOX2 silencing in human hepatocytes alters cellular lipid composition.
  13. RBFOX2 deficiency in human hepatocytes increases expression of ABCA1, ABCG8, NR1H2, and NR1H3, indicating compensatory upregulation of cholesterol efflux pathways due to cholesterol accumulation.
  14. High-fat diet-fed liver-specific RBFOX2 knockout mice show increased levels of specific intrahepatic bile acids.
  15. Liver overexpression of RBFOX2 reduces hepatic cholesterol levels.
  16. RBFOX2-deficient hepatocytes display increased lipid accumulation.
  17. Expression of the short OSBPL9 isoform in wild-type hepatocytes increases levels of ceramide, triglyceride, phosphatidylcholine, and diacylglycerol species similar to those observed in RBFOX2-deficient hepatocytes, indicating a role in lipid metabolism.
  18. In liver-specific RBFOX2 knockout mice, decreased cholesterol and phospholipid content in HDL is restored by reverting the SR-BI to SR-BII isoform ratio, confirming this splicing switch contributes to RBFOX2's role in liver metabolism.
  19. Liver inactivation of RBFOX2 increases biliary cholesterol secretion.
  20. Transient overexpression of RBFOX2 reduces biliary cholesterol secretion.
